About & Technical Architecture

Design, data provenance, multithreaded processing, and analytical methodology

1. Project Purpose

The Indian Cities Population Analytics System is a production-level data engineering and visualization application. It provides an automated, resilient pipeline that extracts municipal population data for Indian urban centers from public sources, cleanses and standardizes the records using concurrent thread execution, stores the normalized data in a relational database with strict constraints, and surfaces factual demographic analytics via REST APIs and dynamic Chart.js dashboards.

2. Data Provenance & Census Limitations

Important Data Integrity Note: The population figures presented in this application are derived from the official Census of India 2011 (conducted by the Office of the Registrar General and Census Commissioner of India). They represent legal municipal corporation boundaries at the time of the 2011 decennial census, NOT current metropolitan agglomeration figures or present-day estimates.
  • Historical Baseline: The decennial Census of 2021 was postponed; therefore, official statutory city-level counts reflect the 2011 survey. We strictly refrain from inventing or interpolating synthetic "current" estimates.
  • Municipal vs. Metropolitan (Agglomeration): Figures reflect the core civic municipal limits (e.g., Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai), which differs from wider Urban Agglomerations (e.g., Mumbai Metropolitan Region).
  • Source URL: Wikipedia: List of cities in India by population

4. Multithreading Rationale & Thread Safety

The pipeline employs concurrent.futures.ThreadPoolExecutor to accelerate processing of scraped records:

  • What is Parallelized: Individual row text cleaning, regex normalization of population numbers, symbol stripping, and preliminary format validation.
  • Why Threading: In Python, I/O-bound tasks and mixed string operations benefit from thread pool concurrency. Workers execute independently without mutating shared data structures.
  • Thread Safety: Worker functions are strictly pure functions—they accept an isolated row tuple and return a new dictionary. Database connections are NOT shared across threads; database writes occur in controlled batches on dedicated session scopes.
  • Multiprocessing vs. Multithreading: While threading suffices for thousands of text rows due to minimal CPU overhead, larger multi-million row datasets would transition to ProcessPoolExecutor to circumvent Python's GIL (Global Interpreter Lock).
